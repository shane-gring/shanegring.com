## What a business operations consultant does

**A business operations consultant maps how a business
actually runs, builds the process and systems that let it run
without the owner in the room, and hands over the
documentation so the team can keep running it.**
The test is whether it still works a month after the
consultant has gone.

That last part is the one that gets skipped. A small business
has no operations department to hand the result to. If the
process only lives in the consultant's notes, or only the
consultant knows how the system works, you have hired a
dependency, and the first time something changes you are back
on the phone with them.

So the order I work in is plain. Write down how the business
makes its calls first. Then build the process and systems to
follow what is written. Then hand it over: the documentation,
the logins, the notes. The writing-down step is the one
people want to skip, and it is the one that decides whether
the rest holds up. There is more on why in
[the approach](https://shanegring.com/approach).

## Business process consultant vs. business systems consultant

The two titles get used interchangeably, but they describe
different halves of the same job.

### Business process consultant

Maps and improves how work moves: the steps, the
handoffs, the decisions. The deliverable is clarity on how
the business makes its calls, written down so it can be
examined and handed to someone else.

### Business systems consultant

Builds or chooses the tools and structure those steps run
on: the software, the accounts, the automation that carries
the process once it is written down.

In practice the two are rarely separable. A process without a
system to run it drifts back to however someone remembers
doing it last time. A system built before the process is
mapped just automates the wrong thing, faster. My work covers
both, in that order: map first, build second.

## Business process automation consulting

Once a process is mapped, some of its steps are worth handing
to software. Business process automation consulting is the
part of this work that finds those steps and builds the
automation on tools and accounts the business owns —
not a separate engagement, the next step of the same one.

It only works once the process itself is written down.
Automating a process nobody has documented just makes the
undocumented version run faster, including its mistakes. The
rule of thumb I use: if a task follows steps you could write
on one page and a wrong answer is cheap to catch, it is a
good candidate. If it turns on judgment, trust, or a
relationship, keep a person on it.

## Who this is for

The work is the same for a small business operations
consultant as for a larger engagement; the dose is smaller.
Fewer processes to map, fewer systems to touch, and usually
the owner directly rather than a layer of managers in
between.

The clean fit is a **founder-led or expert-led business
between roughly $500K and $10M in revenue** — big enough
that the founder can no longer hold every process in their
head, small enough that a full operations department is
premature. If every decision still routes through you and
the knowledge that runs the business lives in a few people's
heads, that is the gap this work fills.
