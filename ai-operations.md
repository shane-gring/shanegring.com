## What an AI consultant for small business should do

**An AI consultant for a small business finds the repeated
work that software can now do, builds it on accounts and code the
business owns, and teaches the team to run and change it.**
The test is whether it still works a month after the consultant
has gone.

That last part is the one that gets skipped. A small business
has no IT department to hand the result to. If the automation
lives in my accounts, or only I know how it works, you have hired
a dependency, and the first time it breaks you are back on the
phone with me.

So the order I work in is plain. Write down how the business
makes its calls first. Then build the automation to follow what
is written. Then hand it over: the repository, the logins,
the notes. The writing-down step is the one people want to skip,
and it is the one that decides whether the rest holds up.
There is more on why in [the approach](https://shanegring.com/approach).

## What to automate, and what to leave alone

The rule of thumb I use: if the task follows steps you could write
on one page, and a wrong answer is cheap to catch, hand it to
software. If it turns on judgment, trust, or a relationship, keep
a person on it.

### Good first jobs for AI

Drafting in your voice from your own material. Pulling the
same numbers into the same report every week. Sorting an inbox
into what needs you and what does not. Researching a prospect
before a call. Keeping a section of your website current.
These are the agents on the menu for [the Install](https://shanegring.com/install), because they are small, they repeat, and you can
see at a glance when one gets it wrong.

### Leave these with a person

Pricing calls. Hiring and firing. Anything a client would be
upset to learn a machine decided. And any process nobody has
written down yet, because an agent handed a vague process does
the vague thing faster.

If you already know the three or four tasks you keep doing by
hand, you are further along than most. If you do not, that is
what the first conversation is for.

## Hiring an AI automation consultant

An AI automation consultant builds the links between your tools
so work moves without someone copying it across. That is useful,
and it is also where a lot of money goes to waste. Before you hire
anyone, including me, ask four things:

- Whose accounts will it run in? The answer should be yours.

- Where does the code or setup live, and can I read it? It should be in a repository you own.

- Who fixes it when it breaks, and how will I know it broke?

- Will my team be able to change it without calling you?

If the answers are vague, you are buying a monthly bill for
something you cannot see into. My answers are on the pages: the
Install ends with the code in your GitHub account and your hands
on the keyboard, and [the Autopilot](https://shanegring.com/autopilot) is
there for anyone who would rather someone else keep it running.

## Top AI consulting firms vs. a one-person practice

I am not going to rank firms here. I have not worked inside most
of them, and a list from someone selling a rival service is not
worth much. What I can tell you is what each kind of help gives a
small business, so you can judge which one fits.

### A large consulting firm

**A team, and depth behind it.** A firm can put
several people on your work at once, cover many specialties,
and keep going if one person leaves. It will have the contracts,
insurance, and process a procurement team expects. It is also
built and priced for companies with more going on than most
small businesses have. Ask who will do the day-to-day work, and
whether you will meet them before you sign.

### A one-person practice (me)

**The person you talk to is the person who does the
work.** I hold the whole picture of your business myself,
my prices are published, and you can start with a free Scan or a
$250 call instead of a proposal. The trade-offs are real: I
cannot staff ten people, I take on a limited number of clients
at once, and if I am out, the work waits.

**When a firm is the better choice:** many locations
or systems that need changing at the same time, heavy compliance
rules, or a need for a team on site for months. **When one
operator is the better choice:** a founder-led business
small enough that one person can understand all of it, where you
want to end up running the tools yourself.

[TeamBuildr](https://shanegring.com/work/teambuildr) is a
strength-and-conditioning platform used by 5,500+ organizations.
Its marketing team had grown from the inside, and months of
top-of-funnel work had piled up in the backlog.

I was brought in to lead the change. We reshaped the team around
the skills already there, built AI into the team's actual
workflows with the brand's standards as the guardrails, and

The reorganizing and the AI were one piece of work, not two.
Adding the tools without changing how the team was set up would
have just made the old way faster.
