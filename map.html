The real story of your business lives in your head.

That's why your best deals still need you in the room. Why your website says something adjacent to the truth. Why every new hire, contractor, and agency has to be re-taught what the company actually is. And why, when an AI answers a question about your business, it works from the weakest version of your story: the one that happened to be written down.

The story was never the problem. The problem is that it was never extracted, structured, and put where the business could use it.

What you get

Three working meetings, 90 minutes each

Not discovery calls. Session one: what the business is now, not what it was when the website was written. Session two: how the work happens, where the leverage is, and where AI and automation belong. Session three: the story, sharpened until it's true and repeatable.

The Operating Memo

The deliverable everything else hangs on: your business logic, your messaging architecture, the structure your web presence should have, the spec for making it machine-readable, and an operations note on the tooling and automation gaps we found. Written to be useful to you forever, whether or not we ever build anything together.

A fixed-price build proposal, as the memo's final page

If the Map points toward a rebuild, you'll have exact scope and an exact number. No follow-up pitch meeting, no "let's hop on a call to discuss pricing." It's a page. Read it or don't.

What the memo looks like

Thirty to forty pages, written to be read by you, your next hire, and whoever builds the thing. The last page is the proposal.

Who this is for

Founder-led businesses, roughly $1M to $10M, where the gap between how the company runs and how it presents has started costing real money. Most people arrive here one of two ways: through the Read, or because they're staring down a website rebuild and suspect the problem is deeper than design.

If you've had a Read

Your Read fee applies in full when you book within 30 days.

If you haven't

You don't need one. The Map includes everything the Read covers and goes several layers deeper.

Whether this is for you

The Map is a poor fit more often than it is a good one, and both of us find that out faster if the list is on the page.

A good fit

  • Founder-led or expert-led, with revenue and a team
  • The way the work actually gets done lives in two or three heads
  • You want the operating layer built, not a better-looking brochure
  • A decision-maker will sit in all three sessions, not send a deputy

Not the right fit

  • What you want is a visual refresh of the site you have
  • The real problem is demand, not operations
  • Nobody inside the business thinks anything needs to change
  • You want the memo, but you do not intend to act on it

Two things to know before booking

The credit

100% of the Map fee applies to a build started within 60 days of your memo. If we build, the Map was free. If we don't, you own the memo and it was worth it on its own. The deal is structured so I have no incentive to write a memo that manufactures a build.

The cap

I run at most four Maps a month, because each one gets my actual attention, not a junior's or a template's. If the month is full, you book the next one. The calendar shows real availability.

Questions

  • Is this a website project? No. It's an operating clarity project that usually reveals the website carries a fraction of the business. Some clients proceed to a build; others fix what they can with their existing setup, using the memo as the spec. Both are fine outcomes.
  • We already have brand strategy docs. How is this different? Brand strategy describes how you want to be seen. The Map documents how you actually run, then derives the story from that. It's the difference between aspiration and extraction.
  • Can my partner / CMO / ops lead join the sessions? Yes, and usually should. The founder is required; up to two others are welcome. More than that and the sessions turn into meetings, and meetings don't extract anything.
  • What happens to the AI and automation findings? Session two surfaces where tooling and automation would pay off, and the memo includes a ranked list with rough costs. If a build happens, that work gets scoped in. If not, the list stands on its own.
  • What if I just want the build? Every build starts with a Map. It's how I scope honestly and how you avoid rebuilding the wrong thing. No exceptions, including for people who offer to pay more to skip it.
