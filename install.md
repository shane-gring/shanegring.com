## Five things, one day, in order.

### 1. Claude Code, installed and authenticated

On your machine, not a demo of mine. Running, logged in,
connected to your work. If a terminal isn't your thing, we set
up the desktop app instead. Both work.

### 2. Your site in version control

Your website's code pulled into a GitHub repository you own.
Every change from here forward is tracked, reversible, and
reviewable. This is the single highest-leverage hour of the day,
and it's the one nobody does on their own.

### 3. Claude connected to the repo

Claude reads your actual codebase. Not a description of it,
not a paste of one file. It sees the whole thing and can change
it.

### 4. Your first change, live

You describe a change in plain English. Claude writes it. You
review it. It ships. You do this yourself, with me watching,
before the day ends. **The point is that you've done it
once, so you believe you can do it again.**

### 5. One agent, built and running

We pick this on the prep call from a short menu, so the
afternoon is building instead of deciding. Options include a
content agent that drafts in your voice from your own material,
a research agent for prospects or competitors, a reporting agent
that pulls your numbers on a schedule, an inbox triage agent, or
a site agent that keeps a section of your website current.

## Questions

**Do I need to know how to code?** No. You need to
be able to follow instructions and not panic at a terminal
window. If a terminal is genuinely off the table, we use the
desktop app and the day works the same.

**What if my site is on Squarespace or Wix?** Then
the GitHub step looks different. We export what's exportable and
set up the repo around it, or we stage a static version. We work
this out on the prep call, not on the day.

**Can I bring my team?** Two people maximum, and
one of them drives the keyboard the entire day. Three or more
